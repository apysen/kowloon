# Dialogue voice guide

The cast should share a place and history, not one speaking rhythm. Avoid giving
several characters the same `question / flat denial / dry correction` exchange.
Reserve clipped defensiveness for Grandfather and brusque commands for Chiu.

- **Mei:** observant, curious, and increasingly direct. She notices concrete
  details and admits what frightens or interests her. Dryness is occasional,
  mostly with close friends.
- **Mum:** thinks in tasks and complete sentences. Care comes through food,
  planning, and making things safe. Under pressure she becomes earnest rather
  than sarcastic.
- **Grandfather:** terse, stubborn, indirect about affection, unexpectedly
  poetic about places and memory.
- **Kit:** quick, expansive, confident, and playfully familiar with Mei. Her
  jokes invite Mei in instead of pushing her away.
- **Wai:** breathless explanations, improvised logic, proud of shortcuts, easily
  distracted. He sounds like a child trying to stay ahead of trouble.
- **Lau:** talkative and self-conscious. He wanders into anecdotes and complains
  theatrically, especially about his clinic.
- **Ho:** literal and mechanical. He thinks aloud in causes, sounds, pressure,
  wiring, and repairs; humor comes from treating people like faulty systems.
- **Ng:** patient, sparse, and reflective. His silences feel calm rather than
  withholding; birds provide his metaphors.
- **Mrs. Chan:** domestic momentum—washing, buckets, Wai, stairs. Affection and
  irritation arrive in the same busy sentence.
- **Mrs. Wong:** formidable, direct, and privately tender. Her sharpest sparring
  is reserved for Grandfather.
- **Kwok:** conversational, gossipy, and fond of stories that take the scenic
  route.
- **Mrs. Cheung:** organized and purposeful, but not hostile. She thinks in
  names, records, and how people will find one another later.
- **Mr. Cheng:** formal and plan-led. As Mei shows him the lived building behind
  the drawing, his language becomes less bureaucratic.
- **Chiu:** economical worksite commands. His brevity reflects concentration,
  not the whole game's default tone.

Before adding a line, read the conversation without speaker labels. If two or
more speakers could trade lines without sounding wrong, revise the wording,
sentence length, or emotional tactic.
